Measured impacts vs FixMyStreet reports

iPhone 11 Pro Max, 2026-10-03_17-30-29 · FixMyStreet data fetched 03 Oct 2026 · Open the comparison map

Overview

What was compared. The 40 impacts my handlebar-mounted phone measured on this ride (jolts of 1 g or more while moving) were checked against FixMyStreet, the public website where people report road problems to the council. Only road-surface reports (potholes, carriageway defects, manholes, drain covers) made in the 365 days before the ride were used.

What was found.

  • 38 of 40 impacts had no FixMyStreet report within 25 m, including all 6 of the strong ones (≥ 1.5 g).
  • 10 of 11 hotspots (places hit repeatedly) had never been reported in that year.
  • The only reported hotspot (3 impacts, up to 2.68 g, on a road outside the study roads) sits 6 m from a pothole report made January 2026 and now marked “Fixed”; it was still producing strong impacts at the time of the ride, so the repair may have failed or a separate defect is there.
  • Of the 10 recent reports along the route, only 2 had an impact measured nearby; all are now closed or fixed, so many were probably repaired before the ride.

What it means. The road hazards a scooter rider actually feels are largely missing from the public reporting system, so FixMyStreet alone underestimates the risk on these roads. Measured impacts were reported no more often than random points along the route (5% vs 14%); with 40 impacts from one ride this shows the gap, not a firm rate.

How to read the details. An impact counts as reported when a FixMyStreet road-surface report (pothole, carriageway defect, manhole, drain ironwork) made in the 365 days before the ride lies within 25 m, whatever its status now. Older reports are left out of the main comparison because they are so dense (back to 2009) that almost any point on the route is near one. The baseline is the same rate for every point along the ridden route: what chance alone would give.

Limitations

  • FixMyStreet only covers reports made through FixMyStreet and its council versions; problems reported to the council by phone or other channels are missing.
  • Report status is as of the fetch date. A report closed or fixed before the ride may not match the surface the rider met; one opened after the ride cannot have been known at the time.
  • “Not reported” means no report within the matching radius, not that nobody ever reported it.
  • Impacts include kerbs, drain covers and speed humps; not every unreported impact is a defect.
  • One ride, one rider; see the statistics report for the impact method and its limits.
