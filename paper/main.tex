\title{Pothole impacts on rental e-scooters and the case for a larger front wheel:\\
       measurements in Oxford and a dynamics model}
\author{Kai Yi Ji}
\date{Draft, \today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent Rental e-scooters are the only e-scooters allowed on British roads. London requires
their front wheel to be at least 11~in across, but the national rules for trials elsewhere set
no minimum. We looked at the Voi Voiager 5, whose wheels are reported to be 10~in, in two
ways. First, we recorded three legal rides in Oxford (6.08~km in total) with a smartphone
fixed to the handlebar. It picked up 100 sharp jolts, each at least as strong as the pull of
gravity (1~g), or about one every 61~m. Ten places jolted the scooter on more than one ride,
and most of them had not been reported as potholes by the public in the year before. Second,
we ran a computer simulation of a scooter and rider crossing potholes, comparing the current
wheels with a bigger front wheel only and with bigger wheels front and back. With the
Voiager 5's front suspension, the simulated rider fell in 9 of 81 runs with 10~in wheels, in 6
with a 12~in front wheel and in 1 with a 14~in front wheel. A bigger front wheel on its own
did as well as bigger wheels at both ends in every pothole tested, which supports our
hypothesis that the front wheel matters most. These results come from one rider and a
simplified model that has not been checked against physical tests; they do not show that
today's scooters are unsafe, and the 1~g level is our own threshold, not a danger limit.
They do support extending a front-wheel minimum to trials outside London, and testing it
with real scooters.
\end{abstract}
