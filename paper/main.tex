\title{Pothole impacts on rental e-scooters and the case for a larger front wheel:\\
       measurements in Oxford and a dynamics model}
\author{Kai Yi Ji}
\date{Draft, \today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent A small front wheel drops further into a pothole and strikes its far edge harder,
which may make e-scooters more likely to fall. We asked whether a larger front wheel would
help, using the Voi Voiager 5 rental scooter, whose tyre markings show an 11.1~in front wheel
and a 10.1~in rear wheel. First, we recorded three legal rides in Oxford (6.08~km in total)
with a smartphone fixed to the handlebar. It picked up 100 sharp jolts, each at least as
strong as the pull of gravity (1~g), or about one every 61~m. Ten places jolted the scooter
on more than one ride, and 6 of these 10 had no public road-surface report within 25~m in the
year before. Second, we ran a physics simulation (MuJoCo) of a scooter and rider crossing
potholes, comparing the Voiager 5's wheels with a smaller 10~in reference, with bigger front
wheels only and with bigger wheels front and back. With the Voiager 5's front suspension,
the simulated rider fell in 9 of 81 runs with its 11/10~in wheels, as often as with 10~in
wheels, but in 6 with a 12~in front wheel and in 1 with a 14~in front wheel. A bigger front
wheel on its own matched bigger wheels at both ends in every case of the main set of runs,
and stayed within one fall of it when the pothole edges were sloped, which supports our
hypothesis that the front wheel matters most. These results come from one rider and a
simplified model that has not been checked against physical tests; they do not show that
today's scooters are unsafe, and the 1~g level is our own threshold, not a danger limit.
They suggest that front wheels larger than the Voiager 5's, around 12--14~in, deserve
physical testing, and that current wheel-size rules for rental trials (11~in in London,
none elsewhere in Britain) may be set too low to make a difference.
\end{abstract}
